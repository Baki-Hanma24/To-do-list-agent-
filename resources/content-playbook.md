# Content playbook: what works for me

Built from my best-performing reel. It's one reel, so treat this as a strong signal, not a law. Update it every time a new reel does well (use `templates/reel-breakdown.md`).

## Best reel so far: "Can I do the high-level dead bug?"

**Results:** 146,255 views · 121,388 viewers · 4.1K likes · 6.2K saves · 1.6K shares · 63 reposts · 27 comments · 84 follows · avg watch 17s of ~46s. A collab with hanma_academy, crossposted to Facebook.

| Signal | Rate | Read |
|---|---|---|
| Saves | ~4.2% of views | Excellent. More saves than likes means people kept it to try later. |
| Shares | ~1.1% of views | Strong. People sent it to training partners, which drove reach. |
| Watch time | ~37% (17s / 46s) | The hook pulled people in, then the long set lost some. |
| Follows | ~0.07% of viewers | Weak. Huge reach, little conversion. The biggest opportunity. |
| Comments | 27 | Low. Nothing asked for a reply. |

**Structure:**
1. **Hook (0-3s): borrowed proof.** A screen recording of a viral Squat University reel: an anatomy animation of the core on top, an athlete's advanced dead bug below, and the text "THIS IS A HIGH LEVEL… DEAD BUG". It sets up a challenge the viewer already finds impressive.
2. **The attempt (3-15s): me trying it.** A hard cut to a cinematic wide shot of me in a dark gym, backlit by a bright open door. I struggle first (roll out, sit up), which makes it honest and relatable.
3. **The payoff (15-46s): I do it.** The full barbell-overhead dead bug, in one continuous set from the same static wide shot.

**Why it worked:**
- **Challenge hook:** "high level" + "can he do it?" creates curiosity in under 3 seconds.
- **Social proof:** the original reel was already viral (about 156K likes), so the hook was pre-tested.
- **Struggle, then success:** a story, not a demo. People stay to see if I get it.
- **Save-worthy:** an advanced core progression people want to try themselves.
- **Shareable:** "try this with me" energy, easy to send to a training partner.
- **Cinematic look:** dark gym, strong backlight, a small figure in a big space. It stands out in a feed of phone-camera clips.
- **Collab:** the hanma_academy collab put it in front of a second audience.

**What to fix next time:**
- **Follows:** tell people why to follow. Make it a series: "Level 1 → Level 5, follow for the next one," "Part 2 on my page."
- **Comments:** ask one easy question ("What level are you at?") or use a keyword ("Comment DEADBUG for the full progression").
- **Watch time:** trim the long set. Cut to the hardest reps, add a quick on-screen rep count or text beat around 10-15s, and end on a clear payoff.
- **Credit:** always tag the original creators (here: @squat_university, @luxiaojunsport, athlete @lidayin89kg) in the caption when using their footage.

## Proven formula (check every reel against this)
- [ ] **Hook in 3s:** a challenge, a "high level" claim, or proof that it's hard.
- [ ] **Story:** attempt → struggle → result, or a progression of levels.
- [ ] **The why:** one line of mechanics or anatomy so it teaches, not just shows.
- [ ] **Save trigger:** something worth trying later (a progression, cues, a program).
- [ ] **Share trigger:** "send this to your gym partner" energy.
- [ ] **Follow trigger:** a series or next part, said or shown at the end.
- [ ] **Comment trigger:** one easy question or keyword.
- [ ] **Cinematic look:** dark, moody gym, strong light source, wide angles.
- [ ] **Credit** any borrowed footage.
- [ ] **Tight:** 30-45s, no dead stretches.
